## Government co-funding and carbon price

Singapore has no capital grant or co-funding scheme for nuclear or for a nuclear-heated hydrogen plant. The 6 Nov 2025 ministerial reply (P19) says no deployment decision exists and mentions no funding. Budget 2026 (P18) names no grant for nuclear or hydrogen capital. The 2022 National Hydrogen Strategy funds R&D only (S$55M awarded, S$129M set aside) and does not mention nuclear (P20). The nearest vehicle is the statutory Future Energy Fund (P21, P22): catalytic, capital-expenditure focused, no fuel or recurrent subsidy, S$5B initial plus S$5B top-up, but no share, cap, instrument or eligible list is stated and nuclear is not linked. The 25/50/75 grid is therefore a policy hypothetical. Carbon tax (P16, P17, P18): S$5/t 2019-2023, S$25 2024-2025, S$45 2026-2027, aim S$50-80 by 2030, no rate published for 2028 onward, Budget 2026 signals the lower end may apply. Real or nominal basis not verified. The S$100/t target exceeds the whole S$50-80 range, so the tax is a comparator and cannot replace a grant. Share support: 25% is supported by UK Net Zero Hydrogen Fund capital grants (30% cap, P4) and Canada's clean hydrogen credit (15/25/40%, P12); Darlington equity is about 14% of C$20.9B (3/20.9, equity not grant). 50% is supported by DOE ARDP demonstrations (P1), the Hydrogen Hubs (P14, snippet only), the US credit at 30+10+10 (P13, my sum) and the EU Innovation Fund ceiling of 60% of net relevant costs (P2). No plant-capital precedent supports 75%. It appears only in R&D grants: UK AMR Phase B (medium firms up to 75%, P5) and DOE risk-reduction awards (up to 80%, P1). Dukovany uses a majority subsidised loan, capped at the funding gap (P11). Eligible items: EU CAPEX categories and UK electrolysis lists (P3, P4). Timing: DOE pro rata per invoice; EU up to 40% before financial close and at least 60% after; UK in arrears on milestones. Interest during construction is excluded by the EU method (P3). Other documents are silent. PDFs were read as extracted text; web pages through the fetch tool.

## Emission factors and physical constants

Seven of the nine targets now have a published value with unit and boundary. Two have none: TS_BURDEN has no published source, and ELECTRICITY_PRICE has none that matches the plant's price basis. Several primary documents (IPCC, UNECE, EMA interactive chart) returned HTTP 403 or empty pages, so those entries are marked accordingly. Confirmed values: - RHO_H2_NORMAL: NIST gives 0.0898825 kg/m3 at 273.15 K and 101.325 kPa (real-gas equation of state), so 0.08988 is correct. The IEAGHG-implied 0.08994 equals the ideal-gas value (my calculation: 101325 x 2.01588e-3 / (8.314463 x 273.15), real-gas Z = 1.0006). At 15 C the ideal value is 0.0853 kg/m3, so the temperature basis matters. - EF_NG_LHV: IPCC 2006 Vol 2 Ch 2 Table 2.2 (printed p. 2.16) gives 56,100 kg CO2/TJ on a net-calorific basis, 95 percent range 54,300 to 58,300. This is CO2 only. IEA quotes about 55 g CO2/MJ for combustion, which agrees. - Carbon tax: NCCS states S$45 per tonne CO2e for 2026 and 2027 and an aim of S$50 to S$80 by 2030, for facilities at or above 25,000 t/y. The presentation notes match. The unit is CO2e, whereas the ledger is CO2. - Methane GWP: AR6 100-year values are 29.8 (fossil) and 27.0 (non-fossil). The 20-year value is about 82.5 (IEA) within a 81 to 83 range (EPA). Use 29.8 for upstream fugitive methane. The IPCC primary was unreachable (403), so these come from the GHG Protocol and EPA reproductions. Values needing relabelling or caution: - GRID_EF 0.402 t/MWh: the EMA Singapore Energy Statistics define the grid emission factor as CO2 only. The 2024 value 0.402 (2023: 0.412) appears only in a search summary of the EMA page, since the fetch returned the chart without values. EMA's data.gov.sg table confirms 0.4080 for 2020. - EF_UPSTREAM 11.5 kg/GJ: the IEA 2026 LNG report attributes 11.5 g CO2-eq/MJ to earlier IEA analysis, as the global average for gas supply by pipeline and LNG. It uses GWP100 = 30 and excludes exploration. The unit must read kg CO2e/GJ. It is a global mix. Delivered LNG averages 18.6, and some Southeast Asian exporters exceed 20. LNG is close to 60 percent of Singapore's supply, so 11.5 probably understates Singapore. - Singapore-specific methane: EMA publishes an upstream fugitive methane factor of 0.00212 kg CH4/kWh for 2020. At GWP100 29.8 this is 0.063 kg CO2e/kWh, about 15 percent on top of the 2020 CO2 factor (my arithmetic). No pipeline-gas intensity for Indonesia or Malaysia was found. - EF_NUCLEAR 5.5 kg/MWh: sits inside the UNECE 2021 range of 5.1 to 6.4 g CO2e/kWh, which I could read only through a law-firm summary. Both UNECE and Warner and Heath (harmonised light-water-reactor median 12 g CO2e/kWh) cover conventional reactors, not a TRISO helium reactor. - Unabated SMR hydrogen: IEA gives 10 to 12 kg CO2-eq/kg H2 (boundary not stated on the page; 75 to 95 percent of it is on-site). Elgowainy et al. 2024 give 9.3 kg CO2e/kg H2 well-to-gate with methane leakage 0.94 percent and GWP 29.8. Cho et al. give 9.35 direct and 11.2 with upstream, both CO2e. The repository's 9.00 to 9.35 comparison rows mix kg CO2 and kg CO2e.

## Financial conventions

Page numbers are printed pages unless marked PDF. Equations were read from extracted text layers; where a layer was garbled this is stated. 1. Definitions. CRF = i/(1-(1+i)^-n) (F1, Eq. 4, pp.1826-1827). LCOE is the constant price that equates discounted revenue with discounted expenses: sum_t E_t P (1+r)^-t = sum_t (Investment_t + O&M_t + Fuel_t + Carbon_t + Decommissioning_t)(1+r)^-t (F3 Eqs. 1, 2, 2', p.34; F2 Eqs. 1-2, p.28, summed from start of construction preparation to end of dismantling). Abatement cost: LCCC = (CRF*dI + dO&M - B)/C, C the annual emission reduction (F1 Eq. 9, reconstructed from a garbled layer). Hydrogen form: CAC = (LCOH_CCS - LCOH_ref)/(CO2_ref - CO2_CCS) per Nm3 H2 (F6 PDF p.10). The repository agrees (crf at anchored.rs:453-461; ledger as annualised difference over tonnes; DCF as NPV ratio). The ratio does not depend on the discount date because both terms are discounted together (derived), so year 0 at end of construction (dcf.rs:5) is harmless. 2. Interest during construction. Overnight cost excludes IDC; investment cost adds it at the same discount rate (F3 p.59; F2 pp.37-38). Spend is uniform, counted from first concrete (F3 p.120). F6 PDF pp.8-11: 8 percent, 25 y, 100 percent debt; TCR = TPC + IDC + owner's costs + spares + working capital + start-up; spend at year end, interest on the previous year-end balance. F4: TOC excludes escalation and IDC, TASC includes both (p.20); funds arrive 1 January, simple interest on the cumulative balance at pre-tax WACC (Eqs. 5-7, Exh. 3-8 to 3-11); real TASC/TOC 1.093 (3 y) and 1.154 (5 y) (p.21). F7 PDF pp.18, 58: nuclear cost inputs mix financing-inclusive and exclusive estimates, not re-baselined. 3. Capital grants. F2 p.29 and F3 pp.36-37: reported costs are social resource costs net of taxes, subsidies and transfers, so a grant leaves the gross cost unchanged. F5: return on investment uses total investment cost before the grant (pp.49-50, 105, 118); return on national capital removes the grant from financed capital (p.50); grant = eligible cost x FGR x co-financing rate, FGR = (DIC - DNR)/DIC (pp.105, 118, 201); interest is never an operating cost (p.45). F9 (secondary): IAS 20 allows deferred income or deduction from carrying amount for asset grants (para 24), recognised once receipt and compliance are reasonably assured; this changes the depreciable base only, which the repository does not use (no tax). No fetched source says whether IDC accrues on the grant-funded share or how timing enters a levelised cost, so D7 must define both. 4. Break-even reporting. The EC funding gap rate is the standard continuous break-even share. Here the analogue is s* with LAC(s*) = 100. For a fixed eligible set and timing, LAC is affine in s (derived), so s* = (LAC0 - 100)/(LAC0 - LAC1), LAC1 at s = 1, and a share passes only above s*. Report gross LAC, net-of-grant LAC and public outlay per tonne. 5. Repository against sources. - Agree: CRF; one real 8 percent for IDC and CRF (parameters.toml:1674, assumed, as F6); IDC capitalised then recovered by CRF; year-end spend (as F6). - Differ, ledger versus DCF: ledger f = ((1+i)^N-1)/(iN), N = 5.5 (economics.rs:232-234, 345) gives 1.19766. Exact continuous uniform spend gives 1.24495, so both increments sit 18 to 19 percent low against the F2/F3 reading.
